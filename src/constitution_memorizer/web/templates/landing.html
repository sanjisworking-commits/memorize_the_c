§ 01

Rereading is recognition. Recall is knowledge.

You read a clause, it feels familiar, and familiarity feels like knowing. But the test is not whether you recognise the words on the page. It is whether you can produce them once the page is gone.

1 On the page

No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.

Every word is right there. Of course you know it.

2 From memory

No person shall be of his or personal except according to established by .

Same line, five words gone. This is the only test that counts.

Recall the C lives entirely in the second panel.

§ 02

Understand the structure. Memorize the wording.

One provision, six modes, each removing a little more of the text. What you read on Monday, you retrieve on Friday.

Article 32(1)Remedies for enforcement of rights

Listening

The right to move the Supreme Court by appropriate proceedings for the enforcement of the rights conferred by this Part is guaranteed.

The exact Bare Act wording, unedited. Read it once, deliberately, then never read it the same way again.

Article 32(1)Cloze

§ 05

The Constitution you memorize has a history.

Wording that looks arbitrary usually is not. Amendment context sits in the margin of the text you are learning, so a clause arrives with the reason it reads the way it does.

§ 07

Before you start

Is the Constitution text paraphrased?

No. Everything you learn is Bare Act wording. Simplification would defeat the point.

What are Visual Explainers?

Diagrams of the relationships inside a difficult provision — branches, conditions, sequences — for the Articles that are easier to see than to read.

Do I need an account to explore it?

No. Browse the Constitution and try the Learn modes as a guest. An account is only needed to save progress and hold your revision schedule.

Are Relevant Laws available?

Not yet. Statutes connected to the Articles around them are upcoming.

Why six Learn modes?

Rereading builds familiarity. Retrieval builds memory — and each mode removes a different kind of support, so the provision is never recalled the same way twice.

Is this a substitute for an official legal source?

No. It is a study and memory aid. Where it matters, verify against official legal publications.

What happens after I learn something?

It enters the revision schedule — 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60 — and comes back to you on its own.

What if I miss a few days?

The queue waits. Overdue provisions come back first, and the schedule re-spaces itself around what you actually remember.
